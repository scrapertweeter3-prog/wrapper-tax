The wrapper tax is rented on every token

A code assistant priced at $20 a month reads like a bargain until the volume shows up. A developer leaning on it for real work generates about 250,000 output tokens a month, which is 250 prompt-response cycles of an average thousand tokens. The subscription covers that. The pay-as-you-go price for the same tokens on a flagship model runs $15 per million output, so the same month bought raw costs $3.75.

The gap is the wrapper tax, and it is bigger than it looks when usage is light. A developer who ships one focused feature a month burns maybe 30,000 output tokens, about $0.45 raw. The subscription still charges $20. At that cadence the flat fee is 44 times the metered cost, which is the most expensive API plan nobody calls an API plan. The pricing only inverts for a heavy user: the 250,000-token month pays a 5.3 times premium, and past about 1.3 million tokens the subscription wins outright.

Scale the same math to a team and the rent compounds per seat. Five developers on the $20 plan cost $1,200 a year. The same five seats metered at their heaviest realistic mix, 400,000 output tokens each per month, bill $360 a year raw, 30 percent of the wrapper price, with no seat minimum and no per-feature upsell gates. The meter always reads lower because the wrapper is selling convenience and a rate card, not the tokens. Where the model spend is audited line by line, the way it is on Pastagi at pastagi.com, the raw bill and the wrapper bill sit side by side and only one of them scales with usage.

Subscriptions price the wrapper, the meter prices the tokens.
